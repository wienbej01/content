# 1. The Master Narrative Progression (The 6-Act Argument)

Every long-form educational video in this format follows a strict 6-Act structure. When generating a storyboard, the script must be mapped to these exact sequential beats.

### Act 1: The Hook & Authority Gap (0% - 5% of runtime)
*   **Beat 1: The Paradoxical Challenge:** Open by stating that a behavior the audience considers "normal" or "smart" is actually causing their failure.
*   **Beat 2: The Macro-Threat:** Introduce an external, urgent force (e.g., industry shift, technology, economic trend) that makes their current failure fatal. 
*   **Beat 3: Extreme Authority Contrast:** The narrator states their credibility by highlighting a massive transformation from a state of extreme disadvantage to extreme elite success.

### Act 2: The Paradigm Tear-Down / Myth Busting (5% - 20% of runtime)
*   **Beat 1: Identify the False Beliefs:** List 3 common myths or default behaviors the audience relies on.
*   **Beat 2: Institutional Proof:** Invalidate these myths using data or case studies from high-status institutions (e.g., Ivy League universities, historical data).
*   **Beat 3: Absolution:** Tell the audience it is not their fault; they were operating on a broken system.

### Act 3: The Core Framework Reveal (20% - 25% of runtime)
*   **Beat 1: Introduce the Architecture:** Reveal a proprietary 4-to-5 part framework (usually an acronym or a tiered categorization).
*   **Beat 2: Visual Map:** Show the full structure visually so the viewer knows exactly what will be covered in the rest of the video.

### Act 4: The Framework Iteration Loop (25% - 85% of runtime)
*This loop repeats for EVERY letter of the acronym or tier of the framework.*
*   **Step A: State the Component:** Introduce the specific step.
*   **Step B: The Anchor Story:** Tell a highly specific, high-status historical, pop-culture, or biographical story that perfectly illustrates this step. 
*   **Step C: The Thesis Extraction:** The narrator explicitly extracts the lesson from the story.
*   **Step D: The Tactical Application:** Show exactly how to execute this step using a modern tool, checklist, or prompt.

### Act 5: The Synthesis (85% - 90% of runtime)
*   **Beat 1: The Recap:** Rapidly review the 4-5 steps.
*   **Beat 2: The Master Tool:** Provide a unified visual or master checklist combining all steps for the viewer to screenshot or save.

### Act 6: The Identity Shift & Call-to-Action (90% - 100% of runtime)
*   **Beat 1: The Philosophical Pivot:** Shift the focus away from the external tactics and onto the viewer's internal mindset, psychology, or identity.
*   **Beat 2: The Final Motivation:** Deliver an empowering, emotionally resonant closing thought.
*   **Beat 3: Soft CTA:** Transition smoothly into the lead magnet or newsletter pitch.

# 2. Visual Asset Allocation (The Shot Mix)

A 15-20 minute video in this format typically contains **250 to 350 total cuts**. The visual assets are allocated strictly by narrative function:

*   **Hero Shots (Presenter): ~35% of runtime**
    *   *Function:* Trust, authority, thesis delivery, and emotional resonance.
*   **Specific/Archival B-Roll: ~35% of runtime**
    *   *Function:* Storytelling, historical proof, and visual anchoring for case studies.
*   **Educational Graphics / UI Overlays: ~15% of runtime**
    *   *Function:* Mapping complex frameworks, displaying lists, and tracking progress.
*   **Metaphorical B-Roll: ~10% of runtime**
    *   *Function:* Visualizing invisible abstract concepts (e.g., cognitive processes, friction, growth).
*   **Kinetic Text Overlays: ~5% of runtime**
    *   *Function:* Pattern interrupts, emphasizing numbers, stats, or key quotes.

# 3. Camera, Framing, and Setting Rules

### The Hero Shot (Narrator)
*   **Framing:** Medium Close-Up (MCU). Cut off just below the chest. Wide enough to capture hand gestures, close enough to read micro-expressions.
*   **Angle:** Direct eye level with the lens. No teleprompter reading eyes.
*   **Setting:** "Intimate Mentor" environment. Seated in a relaxed position (e.g., cross-legged on the floor or in a low lounge chair). Never behind a desk or standing at a podium.
*   **Lighting:** Warm, high-contrast cinematic lighting. Soft key light on the face, with practical lights (lamps, LEDs) in the blurred background providing depth and amber/blue color contrast.

### Triggering the Hero Shot (When to cut to the Narrator)
*   *Rule 1:* Cut to Hero when delivering the core thesis or lesson of a section.
*   *Rule 2:* Cut to Hero when asking the audience a direct, rhetorical question.
*   *Rule 3:* Lock onto the Hero (no B-roll cutaways) during highly vulnerable, emotional, or philosophical monologues (usually in Act 6).

# 4. Supplementary Visuals & B-Roll Taxonomy

DO NOT use generic stock footage (e.g., "business people shaking hands"). B-roll must be categorized and prompted based on its distinct function:

### Type A: Anchor B-Roll (Historical/Specific)
*   **Trigger:** When the script tells a case study or references an event.
*   **Visual Strategy:** Archival photos, vintage footage, or highly specific reenactment footage of the actual subject.
*   **Purpose:** Transfers the high status and undeniable reality of the subject to the abstract framework.

### Type B: Metaphorical B-Roll
*   **Trigger:** When the script explains an abstract internal state (e.g., learning, forgetting, chaos, building).
*   **Visual Strategy:** Physical actions that mirror the cognitive concept. (e.g., Physical friction/combat to represent intellectual friction; collapsing structures to represent failure).
*   **Purpose:** Gives the brain a physical object to associate with an invisible idea.

### Type C: Progressive Educational Graphics
*   **Trigger:** When outlining the 3 Myths, the 4-Part Framework, or a Checklist.
*   **Visual Strategy:** Frosted-glass or clean vector nodes. 
*   **Rule:** *Progressive builds.* Never show a 4-point list all at once. Show Point 1. Then Point 1+2. Then 1+2+3. Highlight the current point being discussed.

### Type D: Tactical Screen Inserts
*   **Trigger:** During the "Tactical Application" phase of the loop.
*   **Visual Strategy:** Screen recordings, UI mockups, or kinetic text mimicking a terminal/chat interface.
*   **Purpose:** Makes the theoretical advice feel immediately executable.

### Type E: Kinetic Typography
*   **Trigger:** A number larger than 1,000, a specific dollar amount, or a profound one-sentence quote.
*   **Visual Strategy:** Center-screen, bold sans-serif text, often animated (e.g., rapid number counting). Short duration (1-2 seconds).

# 5. Pacing and Editing Logic

*   **Pacing by Narrative Phase:**
    *   *During Hook & Storytelling:* High velocity. Cut every **1.5 to 3 seconds**. Constant visual stimulation to build momentum.
    *   *During Framework/Thesis Explanation:* Medium velocity. Cut every **4 to 6 seconds**. Let the educational graphics breathe so the viewer can read them.
    *   *During Emotional/Philosophical Close:* Low velocity. Hold the Hero shot for **10 to 15+ seconds**.
*   **The "Chapter Marker" Transition:** Every time a new section or framework letter begins, use a dedicated title card paired with a subtle audio cue (a low swoosh or soft UI 'tink').
*   **Audio Ducking for Impact:** When delivering a critical paradigm shift or intimate story, drop the background music completely. The sudden silence creates an immediate spike in viewer attention.
