# Background Cat Bible

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Active
**See also:** `UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md`, `JAMES_RECORDING_STUDIO_LIBRARY.md`, `REFERENCE_ASSET_MANIFEST.md`

---

## 1. Principle

The cat is a small, recurring humanizing detail. It signals that this is a real person's space, not a production set. Its presence adds warmth without distraction, and its recurrence across episodes is a quiet continuity signal for attentive viewers.

The cat is background. The cat is occasional. The cat is never a gimmick.

---

## 2. Appearance

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| Breed impression | British Shorthair — dense, round-faced, compact build |
| Fur color | Blue-grey (the classic British Shorthair coloring — solid, medium-dark grey with a slight blue cast) |
| Fur texture | Short, dense, plush |
| Size | Medium; substantial without being oversized |
| Eyes | Copper or golden (consistent with the blue-grey British Shorthair coloring) |
| Distinguishing features | None — the cat is plain and correct, not unusual-looking |

**The cat must look identical every time.** This is a continuity rule, not a preference. If a new generation session produces a differently-colored or differently-shaped cat, it must not be used until it matches the established reference asset (`CAT_LIBRARY_SLEEPING_001` or `CAT_WINDOW_001` in `REFERENCE_ASSET_MANIFEST.md`).

---

## 3. Temperament

The cat is calm, self-possessed, and completely indifferent to the camera and to James's work. It is not curious about the filming. It is not watching James. It is doing what cats do: sleeping, sitting, or moving slowly through its own space.

The cat's temperament must never read as:
- Reactive to events (no startled look, no alert-ear posture toward camera)
- Playful or energetic
- Needy or attention-seeking
- Comic

---

## 4. Typical Locations

The cat may appear in any of these positions within the studio/library:

| Position | Description |
|---|---|
| Sleeping on a chair | A chair to the side or behind James; cat is curled or stretched, fully at rest |
| Near base of bookshelf | Sitting or settled at the foot of a shelf, in the deep background |
| Windowsill | On the sill, looking out; can be used when window is visible in shot |
| Curled near books | On a low shelf or on the floor beside stacked books |

The cat is always in the **deep background** or the **far edge of the frame**. It is never in the near foreground or between the camera and James.

---

## 5. Typical Behaviors

| Behavior | Notes |
|---|---|
| Sleeping or deeply resting | Most common. Eyes closed or half-closed. |
| Sitting still, facing away or sideways | Looking at the window or at nothing in particular |
| Slow movement in background | Walking calmly behind James during a long shot; not brisk |
| Brief visible moment, then out of frame | Acceptable; the cat does not need to stay in frame for a full shot |

---

## 6. Frequency of Use

- The cat should appear in **at most 1 in 3–4 episodes**, not every video.
- When it appears within an episode, it is typically visible in **one or two shots only**, not throughout.
- The frequency is low enough that its presence feels like a discovery, not a formula.

---

## 7. When Not to Use the Cat

- In a high-stakes or very serious topic (financial crisis analysis, high-risk career decision framing). The cat adds warmth; that warmth must not undercut gravity.
- In a teaser or trailer unless the tone specifically allows it.
- In any episode where the cat cannot match the approved reference appearance. If consistency cannot be guaranteed, omit the cat.

---

## 8. How Subtle It Should Be

If a viewer notices the cat on the first watch, its use may be correct. If the cat draws the eye before James does, it is too prominent. The cat should be the kind of thing a viewer mentions to a friend on a second watch: "Did you notice there's a cat in the background?"

A practical rule: the cat's occupied area should be **less than 5% of the frame area**, and it should have **lower luminance or contrast** than James. It sits in shadow or soft ambient light, not in a spotlight.

---

## 9. Forbidden Cat Behaviors

| Behavior | Why forbidden |
|---|---|
| Moving quickly or jumping | Disrupts the frame; creates unwanted comedy |
| Looking directly at camera | Breaks the fourth wall in a way that draws attention |
| Being stroked or interacted with by James on camera | Makes the cat a plot point |
| Appearing in every shot of an episode | Becomes a gimmick |
| Appearing in the foreground | Visual hierarchy error |
| Meowing or vocalizing (implied or actual) | Comic distraction |
| Appearing during a serious, high-stakes moment | Tonal mismatch |
| Looking different from established reference | Continuity failure |

---

## 10. Continuity Rules

1. The cat's appearance (fur color, build, face) must match `CAT_LIBRARY_SLEEPING_001` or `CAT_WINDOW_001`.
2. If a new Higgsfield session generates a cat that is a different color or build, it must not be used.
3. The cat is always in the studio/library environment. It does not appear in city exteriors or other b-roll environments.
4. The cat's position must be plausible within the established studio layout.

---

## 11. Cat Failure Conditions

| Failure | Category |
|---|---|
| Cat has different fur color or breed impression | Continuity |
| Cat is in the foreground or prominently lit | Placement |
| Cat appears in a forbidden behavior | Behavior |
| Cat appears in every episode or every shot of an episode | Overuse |
| Cat appears in an environment other than the studio/library | Environment |
| Cat draws viewer attention before James does | Visual hierarchy |
