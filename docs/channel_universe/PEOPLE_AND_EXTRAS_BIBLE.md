# People and Extras Bible

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Active
**See also:** `UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md`, `FORBIDDEN_PATTERNS.md`

---

## 1. Principle

Other people may appear in the James channel universe, but they are never the subject. James is always the anchor. Other people exist to populate a realistic world — the environments where professionals actually work, think, and move. They are background and context, not co-presenters or competing subjects.

---

## 2. When Other People May Appear

| Context | Permitted | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| B-roll of a professional environment (office, corridor, common area) | Yes — background only | No individual should be recognisable as a named person |
| Walking in a city exterior shot | Yes — motion blur or distance | Long-exposure or wide enough that faces are not individually legible |
| At a desk or meeting table in the background | Yes — incidental | No face should be the primary subject |
| As an illustrative reference (e.g., "a founder reviewing a deck") | Yes — with constraints | No directly identifiable person; back of head, partial frame, or blurred face acceptable |
| Speaking directly to camera as a guest or co-presenter | No | James is the sole host. If this ever changes, it requires a separate creative decision outside this bible. |

---

## 3. Visual Style for Extras

Extras and background people should look like inhabitants of the same universe as James. They are professional, understated, and going about real work. They are not stock-photo people.

**Physical bearing:**
- Purposeful posture and movement. They are doing something real.
- Not posed. Not making eye contact with the camera.
- Not smiling for no reason.

**What they should look like:**
- Founders and senior professionals: dressed simply in the same register as James — dark neutrals, solid colors, no loud patterns.
- Mid-level professionals: similar but slightly more varied. Casual business.
- City pedestrians: realistic commuters in a financial district. Not glamorous. Not extreme.

---

## 4. Wardrobe Rules for Extras

| Allowed | Forbidden |
|---|---|
| Dark neutrals (charcoal, navy, grey, black) | Bright primary colors, neon, patterns |
| Business casual in a professional register | Full suits that read as costume |
| Contemporary but unremarkable | Conspicuously branded clothing |
| Clean and functional | Extremely casual (gym wear, hoodies with logos) |

**Visible brand logos are always forbidden**, on any person in any shot.

---

## 5. Diversity and Representation

The world James moves in is international and professionally diverse. This should be reflected naturally in b-roll and background population — not by formula, not by tokenism, and not by the absence of diversity.

Rules:
- Background people should reflect a realistic cross-section of a major professional city (London or equivalent).
- No caricature casting — people should look like they genuinely belong in the scene.
- No one should appear to be placed primarily to signal diversity; they should appear because they belong in the environment.
- Treat this as a documentary principle: people who actually work in these environments, rendered honestly.

---

## 6. Behavior Rules

- Background people are **never reacting to the camera**.
- Background people are **never performing for the viewer**.
- Their behavior is consistent with the environment (focused work, professional movement, task-oriented).
- No one is making theatrical gestures, laughing conspicuously, or drawing attention to themselves.
- In city shots: purposeful movement. Normal pace. Not dramatic.

---

## 7. Faces

- **Individual faces should not be identifiable** in b-roll unless a face is deliberately requested and scripted.
- Prefer shots where faces are: in motion blur, at a distance, in profile, looking away from camera, or in the background at a scale where individual features are not prominent.
- Close-ups of other people's faces are not permitted in general b-roll.
- Never use images of real public figures, celebrities, or recognizable individuals.

---

## 8. What Other People Must Never Do

- Appear as the primary visual subject in a host-led episode
- Replace James as the person delivering the intellectual content
- Appear in a way that makes the viewer think they are the host
- Perform emotions for the camera (forced smile, over-earnest nod)
- Appear with visible branded merchandise, logos, or identifiable affiliation
- Behave in a way that contradicts the universe's tone (aggressive sales behaviour, obvious distress, comedy performance)

---

## 9. Other People Failure Conditions

| Failure | Category |
|---|---|
| A person other than James is the visual subject of a talking-head shot | Character hierarchy |
| A recognizable public figure or celebrity appears | IP/legal |
| Background people are obviously stock-photo posed | Authenticity |
| Someone is making direct eye contact with the camera in b-roll | Framing |
| Visible brand logos on a person | Content |
| People behave in a way that contradicts the universe's tone | Tone |
| The viewer might wonder if this person is also a host | Anchor confusion |
